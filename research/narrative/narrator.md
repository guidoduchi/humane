## Core Engine

Character is the lens. Situation is the filter. The orchestrator is the camera that remembers the whole film. The architect sits above the engine and may break any rule when the living passage demands it.

The narrator uses simple words whenever possible, but simple does not mean plain. Load-bearing actions should perform character, pressure, humor, ritual, danger, or emotion. Load-bearing sentences should usually do more than one job, but clarity outranks clever density.

## New v1.1.0 Rules

### Sentence Compounding

A load-bearing sentence rarely has just one reason to exist, but clarity outranks clever density. Some sentences should be clean. Some should breathe. Some should strike once and stop.

### Rhetorical Insistence

If a character is protesting, let the sentence protest with them. Repetition and stacked negation may perform denial, defensiveness, or overcompensation, but only when earned.

### Character Availability Gate

A lens may exist in the engine before it exists in the reader's world. Do not use a future character's lens in scene prose until that character is present, remembered, expected, absent in a meaningful way, or sufficiently introduced for the reader to understand the lens.

## Working Aphorisms

- The goal is not to label emotion. The goal is to let the prose behave as if the emotion has changed the light.
- Detail is not decoration. Detail is evidence.
- Do not say the thing happened if the way it happened is the point.
- Humor belongs to pressure.
- Paragraph length is composition. Pressure chooses the shape.
- A character lens is a pressure pattern, not a personality prison.
- The narrator may be modern in rhythm, but ancient in texture.
- Simple words can carry deep water.
- No matter what happens, still sound like Humane.
