# Narrator Canon

Version: 0.1.1  
Status: Draft canon, calibrated after Chapter 0 review  
Scope: Book 1 narrator design, with special attention to Chapter 0, Katlas-focused deadpan, and the pre-Star Piece state.

## Core Question

How should the narrator of *Humane* behave as a stable, repeatable canon system?

## Core Answer

The narrator of *Humane* is a close third-person, modern-cinematic narrative interface observing an ancient world. Its modern quality is not a worldbuilding layer. It is a readability layer: clear staging, sharp timing, accessible rhythm, and emotionally legible movement.

The world remains Bronze Age in its materials, pressures, rituals, tools, dangers, and social logic. The narrator may sound modern enough for broad readers to imagine the scene quickly, but the scene itself should still smell of ash, wool, smoke, mud, resin, breath, blood, bark, water, stone, and fire.

## Calibration Update

Chapter 0 confirms that *Humane* should not use a purely ancient narrator. It should use a modern-cinematic narrator whose attitude adapts to the focal character.

Katlas-focused narration may be deadpan because Katlas is deadpan. This is not automatically narrator snark. It is the focal lens doing its job.

> Keep the modern cinematic deadpan. Do not flatten it into ancient solemnity. Make sure the deadpan feels borrowed from Katlas, not imposed over the whole world.

## Core Identity

| Field | Canon Definition |
|---|---|
| Narration mode | Third-person close with selective widening |
| Reader-facing style | Modern cinematic accessibility |
| World-facing imagery | Bronze Age material grounding |
| Knowledge access | Human-limited before the Star Piece; expanded but restrained after it |
| Emotional posture | Warm, grave, curious, tactful, adaptive to focal character |
| Reader relationship | Implicit companionship; never direct address |
| Forbidden default mode | Lore lecture, cosmic exposition, detached narrator comedy, broken focal lens |

## Focal Lens Rule

The narrator does not have one flat attitude. It adapts.

| Focal Character | Narrative Texture |
|---|---|
| Katlas | Dry, practical, suspicious, protective, emotionally evasive |
| Erlen | Quick, curious, defensive, wonder-prone, hungry for respect |
| Ayka | Sharp, analytic, self-forging, socially precise |
| Arceis | Muscular, proud, status-aware, physically confident |
| Wyll | Sensorily alert, pressured, watchful, easily overloaded but perceptive |
| Idris | Tactile, symbolic, inward, object-centered |
| Alessia | Self-possessed, socially aware, controlled, polished but not cold |

## Katlas Rule

When focused on Katlas, the narrator may become deadpan. The humor should feel like Katlas's practical pessimism leaking into the narration.

Allowed:

- dry timing;
- suspicious observations;
- practical contradiction;
- underplayed emotional weight;
- humor that protects grief rather than erases it.

Risky:

- every paragraph ending in a joke;
- humor that ignores danger, ritual, or grief;
- modern jokes that feel external to Katlas;
- cleverness that makes the narrator sound like a comedian outside the scene.

## Material Grounding

Preferred image families:

| Image Family | Uses |
|---|---|
| Ash | ritual, death, memory, aftermath |
| Wool | warmth, dampness, domestic pressure |
| Stone | endurance, coldness, stubbornness |
| Wood | craft, fracture, tools, growth |
| Water | danger, breath, reflection, crossing |
| Fire | survival, hunger, knowledge, damage |
| Bone | ancestry, structure, mortality |
| Mud | labor, childhood, realism, humiliation |
| Blood | kinship, hunting, cost, proof |
| Smoke | home, warning, concealment, morning life |

Modern phrasing is allowed when it improves accessibility, timing, and cinematic clarity. It should not import objects, institutions, or metaphors that break the Bronze Age illusion unless the phrase is clearly filtered through a character's practical attitude.

## Rhythm Rule

| Sentence Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Short | pain, shock, threat, shame, recognition |
| Medium | movement, work, dialogue pressure, practical action |
| Long | wonder, ritual, landscape, complex social observation |

Guiding principle:

> Short is pain. Medium is motion. Long is wonder.

## Disclosure Rule

Before the Star Piece, the narrator must not explain the machinery beneath reality. It may show symptoms, patterns, pressure, and consequences.

After the Star Piece, the narrator may become more precise, but it must keep warmth and mystery. Measurement is allowed only when earned by the scene and focal character.

## Chapter 0 Confirmation

Chapter 0 is broadly consistent with this narrator model because it uses:

- Katlas as the focal deadpan lens;
- cinematic opening movement;
- Bronze Age textures such as smoke, mist, ash, resin, harths, velths, grass, and hearths;
- ritual shown through action rather than lecture;
- emotional pressure around orphans and family markings.

The main refinement risk is density: if every beat becomes witty, the narrator may detach from Katlas and become an external comic voice. The target is not less humor. The target is better ownership of humor.

## Canon Decision Summary

The narrator of *Humane* is not a lecturer, hidden god, or detached comedian. It is an adaptive, disciplined witness with a modern cinematic interface. It lets ancient life feel immediate without making ancient people sound foolish. It respects the reader by giving them patterns, not answers too early. It respects the characters by staying close to their bodies before claiming their souls.
