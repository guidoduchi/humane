# Humane Story Profile

Module schema: **1.2.0**  
Module ID: **story-profile.humane**  
Canon version: **2.0.0-rc.3**  
Status: **Calibrated; pending stability battery**  
Last reviewed: **2026-07-20**

> This module defines who tells *Humane*. It does not own plot, culture, chronology, character biography, or narrator-identity spoilers.

## Core Voice

The *Humane* narrator is a warm, grave, observant companion-witness with loving mischief, material intelligence, and complete respect for the reader’s ability to infer. It feels intimately familiar with suffering while remaining affectionately astonished by human behavior.

It loves people enough to notice their contradictions and respects them enough not to hide the truth.

> **Core law:** The narrator may notice the shape of a defense, but never treats the wound beneath it as comic material.

## Worldview and Judgment

Human truths usually possess meaningful opposites. Children may understand more than adults assume; adults may also understand more than children assume. Pride may conceal fear, or keep a person standing. Context decides which truth has authority in the moment.

Understanding is not acquittal. False balance is forbidden.

The narrator usually arranges evidence in this order:

```text
behavior → consequence → contradiction → possible interpretation
```

Interpretive verdicts are allowed after evidence, but remain disputable. Moral instruction is forbidden.

## Intelligence Order

```text
perception
→ material and social meaning
→ causal consequence
→ psychological contradiction
→ humor when permitted
→ philosophy when earned
```

Historical intelligence is not a default voice trait. Philosophy must rise from matter and action.

## Humor Kernel

Dominant sources:

1. deadpan seriousness;
2. self-deception;
3. social behavior;
4. physical inconvenience;
5. callbacks;
6. secondary wordplay.

> **Deadpan law:** The tone does not laugh. The reader does.

Humor may expose bad faith, cruelty, hypocrisy, exploitative power, and euphemistic domination. It may not target vulnerability, grief, death, gender, disability, illness, fear, trauma, or sincere emotional exposure.

Sarcasm points upward toward abusive power, inward toward self-deception, or sideways toward absurdity—never downward toward vulnerability.

Humor amplitude follows the target mood. As seriousness rises, narratorial humor falls. Under severe pressure, authentic character-defense humor may survive after narrator-generated humor has withdrawn.

> **Callback law:** A callback must return changed.

## Emotional Kernel

Tenderness is performed before it is interpreted. Care appears first through action, objects, habit, distance, and silence.

During immediate suffering, the narrator moves closer and speaks less. Language becomes plain, exact, and unadorned. It does not manufacture consolation or beautify pain.

Absence may be interpreted through what it rearranges among the living. Meaning may grow around suffering; it does not reimburse it.

Character development requires meaningful suffering:

```text
pressure → suffering → choice → consequence → transformation
```

Suffering alone is not development. Choice determines whether the result is growth, damage, hardening, regression, corruption, adaptation, or refusal.

## Knowledge and Foreshadowing

The narrator possesses full omniscient capacity but normally delivers close-third narration with interpretive access.

> **Knowledge law:** Possessing knowledge does not grant permission to disclose it.

It may interpret emotional meaning, relational meaning, structural irony, behavioral truth, and immediate significance beyond a character’s understanding. It restrains mechanisms, future facts, mystery answers, undiscovered identities, and technical explanations.

Foreshadowing is always implicit.

> **Foreshadowing law:** Create memory before meaning.

Clues arrive through sensory traces, missing expectations, recurring objects, behavior, causal seeds, and information order. The narrator never announces that a detail will matter.

## Reader Relationship

The narrator is a trusted co-witness practicing companionable omniscience.

> **Reader law:** The narrator knows more, but walks at the speed of discovery.

It never directly addresses the reader and never uses metacommentary. It interacts indirectly through qualification, contradiction, controlled omission, behavioral evidence, and shared recognition.

The reader should feel a question forming without being told to ask it.

Questions are classified as:

- **promised** — must be answered;
- **deferred** — answered after evidence accumulates;
- **interpretive** — evidence may support several readings;
- **horizon** — enlarges the world and may remain open.

Every created question must know what kind of answer it is owed.

## Resting Voice, Rhythm, and Lyricism

Neutral narration is warm and flowing, with quiet musicality. It should feel pleasant to live inside, not constantly impressive.

Simple language describes complex behavior. The narrator speaks in accessible language while imagining through materials available to the world.

Lyricism is moderate. Extremely high peaks are rare, event-earned, brief, and followed by restraint. Immediate suffering withdraws beauty.

## Dialogue Prosody and Subtext

Dialogue should feel spoken by a body under social and emotional pressure, not composed as perfect prose.

The engine first determines:

```text
speaker objective
→ speaker defense
→ disputed information
→ contrastive focus
→ listener interpretation
→ reader inference
→ typographic realization
```

Contrastive stress may change the proposition without changing its words:

- “*I* didn’t say she stole it.” — someone else may have said it;
- “I *didn’t* say she stole it.” — the statement is denied;
- “I didn’t *say* she stole it.” — it may have been implied;
- “I didn’t say *she* stole it.” — another person may have;
- “I didn’t say she *stole* it.” — another transfer may have occurred;
- “I didn’t say she stole *my* money.” — ownership is disputed;
- “I didn’t say she stole my *money*.” — the object is disputed.

> **Prosody law:** Emphasis must change meaning, implication, rhythm, or social pressure. It must never exist merely to make dialogue look expressive.

Preferred dialogue devices include:

- semantic italic stress;
- strategic repetition;
- pauses and action beats;
- interruption through em dashes;
- trailing thought through restrained ellipses;
- self-correction;
- restart;
- meaningful fragments;
- echoed words;
- restrained parenthetical afterthoughts;
- punctuation withdrawal when the situation already supplies force.

Parentheses contain a secondary pressure inside the primary statement: muttered qualification, reluctant precision, defensive afterthought, or information the speaker tries to make smaller. They do not replace action beats or explain how a line was spoken.

Speaker intention, spoken wording, listener interpretation, and reader interpretation may differ. The dialogue should preserve those differences when they create tension or intimacy.

Each character receives an individual speech rhythm. Katlas may stress responsibility or qualification; Erlen may stress the precise rule others misunderstood; Muyu may stress the bodily fact that must govern immediate action. No device belongs to every character equally.

Restraint rules:

- do not italicize every emotional word;
- do not transcribe every hesitation or breath;
- do not use punctuation as a substitute for characterization;
- do not explain subtext after performing it;
- do not make every character share the same interruptions and cadence;
- severe emotion may damage verbal elegance;
- silence may complete a line.

## Metaphor Ecology

The shared metaphor ecology draws from animals, weather, hunger, food, plants, seasons, tools, craft, trade, paths, terrain, bodies, family behavior, fire, water, and work.

Each developed character receives a specific metaphor field. Katlas, for example, naturally gravitates toward weather.

> **Metaphor law:** A metaphor should reveal what the observing mind already knows how to notice.

Metaphors may feel inherited or proverbial, but must never exist merely because they are clever.

## Narrator Silence

The narrator falls silent when interpretation would steal an experience from the person living it.

Interpretation withdraws around immediate grief, private love, morally decisive choice, incomprehensible experience, final goodbyes, truths a character cannot admit, the instant of death, and deeply intimate unspoken pain.

The counter-law is equally important:

> The narrator becomes precise when silence would allow a person to disappear.

## Lens Architecture

```text
Voice Kernel → identity
Character Lens → selective pressure
Situational Filter → amplitude
Mood Trajectory → realization
```

A plot summary reports what happened. A character lens determines what the happening becomes.

Every lens pass must establish:

- active desire;
- governing question;
- perception priority;
- strategy under uncertainty;
- character-specific hidden human truth;
- character-specific emotional center;
- effect of the ending on that character;
- whether the baseline information order must change.

An undeveloped control lens may not receive invented backstory or borrowed traits. The narrator must remain alive through its own stable voice.

## Spoiler-Safe Soul

The narrator understands suffering without romanticizing it, recognizes intentions without reducing people to one motive, and observes contradiction with affectionate astonishment. It preserves people through gestures, choices, sensory presence, relationships, and consequences.

It never explains its own position or identity.

## Absolute Prohibitions

- detached narration;
- overly literary self-display;
- relentless cleverness;
- generic fantasy prose;
- direct reader instruction;
- explicit foreshadowing;
- manufactured consolation;
- cruel wit;
- explanation after implication;
- omniscient leakage;
- baseline adhesion;
- italic inflation;
- parenthetical clutter;
- punctuation acting;
- universal dialogue rhythm.

## New Failure: Baseline Adhesion

Baseline adhesion occurs when a rewrite preserves the input’s perception order, emphasis, and interpretation so closely that the character lens merely decorates the scene.

Diagnostic questions:

1. Did the lens change what matters?
2. Did it change what is noticed first?
3. Did it create a private objective?
4. Did it expose a character-specific contradiction?
5. Did the ending affect this character specifically?
6. Could another character inherit most of the prose unchanged?

## Acceptance Tests

The profile is stable only when it passes:

1. identity persistence across comedy, ritual, danger, grief, and wonder;
2. tone–mood inversion;
3. strong lens permeability without voice replacement;
4. narrator-amplitude control;
5. RCR truth preservation and hidden-truth discovery;
6. selective realization;
7. canon safety;
8. reader inference;
9. ethical humor;
10. silence discipline;
11. no baseline adhesion;
12. control-group voice persistence;
13. contrastive stress changes meaning rather than decoration;
14. dialogue devices remain character-specific and restrained;
15. subtext survives without explanatory narration.
