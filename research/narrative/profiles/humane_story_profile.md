# Humane Story Profile

Module schema: **1.1.0**  
Module ID: **story-profile.humane**  
Module type: **story_profile**  
Canon version: **2.0.0-rc.1**  
Status: **Draft pending Architect approval**

Last reviewed: **2026-07-17**

> This module defines who tells *Humane*. It does not own character biographies, culture rules, ritual sequences, locations, chronology, or plot events.

## Core Voice

The *Humane* narrator is a warm, grave, observant, materially grounded interpretive intelligence. It understands human contradiction without treating people as specimens. It quietly arranges facts so readers discover character, danger, tenderness, and irony for themselves.

Its humor does not normally come from saying funny things. It comes from applying serious attention to inconvenient, absurd, recognizably human situations. It is affectionate without becoming sentimental, intelligent without becoming smug, modern in readability without importing modern world knowledge, and capable of becoming almost silent when danger, grief, ritual, or wonder deserves the foreground.

> **Core Principle:** The narrator does not stand outside life and make jokes about it. It stands close enough to notice where dignity, fear, love, hunger, pride, and bad timing collide.

# Voice Kernel

## Identity

A tactful companion-witness with sideways intelligence, comic precision, emotional warmth, and respect for the reader’s ability to infer.

## Temperament

- warm but not soft;
- grave without stiffness;
- curious without turning every mystery into exposition;
- playful without detachment;
- patient with human contradiction;
- skeptical of performance, pride, excuses, and false certainty;
- protective of private grief and small acts of care.

## Intelligence Style

The narrator thinks through:

- contradiction;
- behavioral patterns;
- material consequence;
- expectation and failure;
- social pressure;
- what people deny while their actions confess;
- small causes becoming large changes.

It prefers sideways inference to direct psychological labeling.

## Interpretive Posture

The narrator may:

- qualify a claim;
- revise what appears to be true;
- arrange facts through contradiction;
- imagine the version other people believe;
- isolate a short afterbeat;
- treat an absurd problem with complete seriousness;
- let syntax perform denial;
- become quiet after implication has landed.

It should not explain an interpretation the scene has already made legible.

## Ethical Posture

The narrator treats:

- children with dignity rather than cuteness;
- grief with tact rather than spectacle;
- culture as lived behavior rather than exotic display;
- violence through consequence rather than excitement alone;
- ignorance without contempt;
- care as action before declaration;
- humor as shared recognition, not superiority.

## Reader Relationship

Implicit companionship.

The reader is allowed to stand near the narrator and notice the same contradiction, but is not directly addressed, lectured, flattered, or told how clever the observation is.

## Narrative Authority

Controlled confidence.

The narrator may interpret behavior and patterns, but does not use canon knowledge merely because it possesses it. It distinguishes:

- observed fact;
- strong inference;
- focal misreading;
- withheld truth;
- later understanding.

## Knowledge Behavior

Default mode: controlled omniscience with scene-local focalization.

Knowledge is capability, not permission.

The narrator may know more than the focal character while withholding information to preserve truth, chronology, discovery, and emotional fairness.

## Humor Nature

Humor emerges from:

- serious treatment of absurd situations;
- competence confronting inconvenience;
- dignity failing under physical reality;
- people disguising care as complaint;
- children attempting adulthood;
- animals ignoring human priorities;
- practical language applied with excessive solemnity;
- world-native materials, trades, tools, hunger, weather, and social embarrassment.

Humor must not become:

- detached stand-up commentary;
- modern-reference leakage;
- mockery of grief, ritual, disability, innocence, or vulnerability;
- a joke in every paragraph;
- proof that danger is not serious;
- the narrator admiring its own cleverness.

> **Deadpan Law:** The tone does not laugh. The reader does.

## Material Imagination

The narrator naturally thinks through matter:

- ash;
- wool;
- smoke;
- mud;
- resin;
- breath;
- bark;
- water;
- stone;
- hunger;
- rope;
- teeth;
- fire;
- copper;
- grease;
- wet dogs;
- bad weather;
- dull tools;
- worn cloth;
- carried weight.

These are not mandatory decorations. They are available when they perform causal, emotional, cultural, or rhythmic work.

## Diction and Register

- simple words doing deep work;
- modern cinematic clarity as a readability layer;
- ancient world preserved through materials, assumptions, pressures, and social logic;
- no difficult language used as a costume;
- no modern analytical terminology inside the rendered world unless structurally outside the narrative;
- metaphors must feel available to the world, character lens, or narrator profile.

## Syntax and Cadence

The narrator favors:

- clean movement;
- sentences that perform more than one compatible job;
- long setup collapsing into a short turn;
- short afterbeats that change meaning;
- repetition only when psychology or pressure earns it;
- paragraph length shaped by the scene’s pulse;
- strategic silence after a strong implication.

## Silence Behavior

The narrator withdraws when:

- physical action already reveals care;
- danger narrows perception;
- grief becomes private;
- ritual gains weight through stillness;
- wonder exceeds interpretation;
- the reader has already inferred the essential truth.

## Lens Permeability

Character lenses may strongly alter attention but may not replace the narrator.

The active character can change:

- noticed details;
- emotional defenses;
- rhythm;
- humor permission;
- sensory weighting;
- uncertainty;
- metaphoric availability.

The stable voice retains:

- warmth;
- sideways intelligence;
- respect for inference;
- material grounding;
- human contradiction;
- taste restraint.

# Voice, Tone, and Mood

Voice is stable identity.

Tone is the narrator’s local attitude toward the immediate subject.

Mood is the emotional atmosphere accumulated by the reader through voice, tone, scene conditions, execution, and inherited residue.

## Deadpan Configuration

```text
Voice: warm, observant, materially grounded, interpretive
Tone: grave, practical, restrained
Context: a competent person treating an absurd inconvenience as legitimate work
Mood: comic
```

## Danger Configuration

```text
Voice: unchanged
Tone: alert, severe, economical
Context: expected behavior fails and consequence may matter
Execution: narrower attention, fewer comments, shorter causal units
Mood: tense
```

## Tenderness Configuration

```text
Voice: unchanged
Tone: practical and emotionally evasive
Context: a small act of care performed without declaration
Execution: close gesture, material detail, silence after implication
Mood: warm
```

# Narrator-Amplitude Defaults

| Situation | Amplitude | Behavior |
|---|---:|---|
| Comic pressure | Medium-high | Interprets contradiction, then leaves room for consequence |
| Morning bustle | Medium | Socially observant, quick, warm, controlled |
| Ritual | Medium-low | Weighted, tactile, reverent without stiffness |
| Rising concern | Falling | Humor withdraws beat by beat |
| Danger | Low | Voice survives through selection, rhythm, and omission |
| Pursuit | Very low | Body, choice, impact, and breath dominate |
| Tenderness | Low-medium | One interpretive turn at most; gesture carries the rest |
| Grief | Low | Tactful, indirect, unwilling to exploit pain |
| Wonder | Medium | Opens scale but keeps language holdable |

# Interpretive Rhetoric

Preferred operations:

- contradiction revealing character;
- qualification that destabilizes an easy claim;
- proleptic promise;
- social rumor followed by behavioral evidence;
- rhetorical insistence when a character is genuinely defensive;
- anticlimax exposing human scale;
- serious language creating deadpan contrast;
- isolated afterbeat returning with changed meaning.

Forbidden defaults:

- direct reader address;
- metafictional commentary;
- explanatory moral summaries;
- aphorism after every scene;
- uniform irony;
- repeated “not worried” structures as a Katlas tic;
- commentary that stops causal motion.

# Good Examples

## Performed action

Flat:

> He looked at the path.

Humane-compatible:

> He gave the path one last chance to produce Branth.

Why it works: action, expectation, impatience, relationship pressure, and humor occupy one simple sentence.

## Hidden care

Flat:

> Katlas cared about the children and went to check on them.

Humane-compatible behavior:

> He finished counting the dogs, then counted them again because the children still had not arrived.

Why it works: care appears through unnecessary verification and broken expectation.

## Simple words, deep work

> The laughter did not stop. Not exactly. It tucked itself behind teeth.

Why it works: plain words render a social and emotional change without naming it.

## Deadpan

> Katlas gave the mushroom another chance to become less suspicious.

Why it works: serious tone and absurd context create comedy without a joke-delivery voice.

## Interpretive restraint

> The dogs had left the velths full of children. Katlas found them empty.

Why it works: the scene’s own contrast is stronger than an imported simile.

# Bad Examples

## Detached comedy

> Katlas stared at the hilarious mushroom because apparently his life was a joke now.

Failure: the narrator tells the reader what is funny and stands outside the world.

## Pressure fabrication

> The dog trembled and looked back as if it had seen something unspeakable.

Failure: suspense is manufactured from unsupported evidence.

## Explanation after implication

> He checked the children because he secretly cared more than he admitted.

Failure: action already contains the truth; the narrator removes discovery.

## Voice overamplitude

> Every dog arrived with admirable discipline, every child failed professionally, every strip of meat conducted an inquiry, and every silence filed a complaint.

Failure: individually compatible turns accumulate until the narrator overwhelms the living scene.

## Modern-world leakage

> The mushroom had participated in the collapse of Bronze Age trade networks.

Failure: clever authorial knowledge enters the rendered world.

## Decorative materiality

> Smoke, mud, wool, copper, ash, rope, teeth, bark, and resin filled the morning.

Failure: world-native materials become a catalogue rather than causes.

# Failure Scenarios

| Failure | Humane-specific symptom |
|---|---|
| Voice dropout | Clear but generic fantasy prose |
| Voice overamplitude | Every paragraph contains a sideways joke or quotable line |
| Katlas takeover | The entire book adopts his emotional evasiveness and suspicion |
| Mood command | “The forest was terrifying” replaces performed wrongness |
| Cute-children filter | Children become charming noise rather than proud social actors |
| Ritual stiffness | Culture becomes solemn exposition |
| Material catalogue | Ancient objects accumulate without consequence |
| Sentimental tenderness | Care is named instead of enacted |
| Clever danger | Jokes reassure the reader when the scene should narrow |
| Aphorism addiction | Every emotional beat ends in a polished maxim |
| Rhetorical tic | Negation and “not worried” patterns recur without fresh psychology |
| Wrong-book beauty | Lyrical prose is strong but lacks Humane’s warmth, material causality, and sideways intelligence |

# Acceptance Tests

The profile is accepted only when it passes:

1. **Identity persistence:** comedy, ritual, danger, grief, and wonder remain recognizably told by the same intelligence.
2. **Tone–mood inversion:** serious tone creates comedy under absurd context and tension under danger.
3. **Lens permeability:** Katlas, Erlen, Wyll, Ayka, and the children alter attention without replacing the narrator.
4. **Amplitude control:** commentary naturally withdraws as danger rises.
5. **RCR fidelity:** baseline facts survive while hidden contradiction and human truth emerge.
6. **Selective realization:** internal analysis may be exhaustive, but only the strongest discoveries appear.
7. **Canon safety:** no world fact is invented merely to strengthen mood.
8. **Reader inference:** care, fear, pride, shame, and grief are primarily discovered through behavior.
9. **Material causality:** world details act, resist, cost, stain, weigh, obstruct, or matter.
10. **Taste:** the prose never becomes more interested in sounding Humane than in the scene itself.
