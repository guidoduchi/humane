# Story Engine

Version: **2.0.0-rc.5**  
Engine schema: **2.0.0**  
Story Profile schema: **1.2.0**  
Status: **Calibrated voice and dialogue runtime**

## Separation of Authority

- **Story Engine:** how stories are transformed and told.
- **Story Profile:** who tells this story.
- **Canon Modules:** what is true.
- **Scene-State Ledger:** what is temporarily true.

## Runtime

```text
Story Profile
→ Voice Kernel
→ Reverse Close Reading
→ Narrative Possibility Map
→ Character Lens + Situational Filter
→ Tone and Mood Trajectory
→ Voice-Amplitude Controller
→ Dialogue Prosody and Subtext
→ Selective Realization
→ Render
→ Verification
```

## Reverse Close Reading

> **RCR law:** Do not decorate the baseline. Discover what the baseline has not yet learned to say.

Stages:

1. preserve truth;
2. extract the causal skeleton;
3. locate latent contradiction;
4. identify hidden human truth;
5. inspect expected patterns;
6. generate reader questions;
7. reconsider information order;
8. convert labels into behavior;
9. identify rhetorical opportunities;
10. ground the scene materially;
11. design the mood trajectory;
12. test risks.

Internal analysis may be exhaustive. Final prose must be selective.

## Lens Recompilation

A plot summary is not a character lens, even when its surface emotion resembles the focal character.

> **Lens law:** A plot summary reports what happened. A character lens determines what the happening becomes.

Every focal pass must establish active desire, governing question, perception priority, strategy under uncertainty, character-specific hidden truth, character-specific emotional center, ending effect, and whether information order must be rebuilt.

The lens must change importance, not merely vocabulary.

## Baseline Adhesion

Baseline adhesion occurs when a rewrite preserves the baseline’s perception order, emphasis, and interpretation so closely that the lens only decorates the scene.

A pass fails when sensory enrichment substitutes for character transformation, no private objective organizes perception, the hidden contradiction remains plot-level, the ending does not affect the focal character, or another character could inherit most of the prose unchanged.

## Voice–Lens Architecture

```text
Voice Kernel supplies identity.
Character Lens supplies selective pressure.
Situational Filter controls permission and amplitude.
Mood Trajectory controls realization.
```

When lens data is sparse, the narrator must preserve its identity without inventing biography or borrowing another character’s traits.

## Narrative Possibility Map

Every discovery receives one status:

- **realize now**
- **imply only**
- **reserve for later**
- **discard**

The strongest internal discovery is not automatically the correct sentence.

## Knowledge and Curiosity

Knowledge is capability, not permission.

The engine distinguishes what is true, what matters now, what the lens perceives, what the narrator may interpret, what must remain unnamed, what sensory evidence the reader can hold, and what question should remain alive.

Questions are promised, deferred, interpretive, or horizon. Every question must know what kind of answer it is owed.

Foreshadowing is implicit. It creates memory before meaning.

## Humor Controller

Humor responds to target mood, stakes, ethical eligibility, and character authenticity.

Narratorial humor approaches zero before authentic character-defense humor does. Vulnerability may never become the comic target.

## Dialogue Prosody and Subtext

Dialogue is not only the sequence of words a character chooses. It is the sequence of pressures placed upon those words.

The engine analyzes dialogue in this order:

```text
speaker objective
→ speaker defense
→ disputed information
→ contrastive focus
→ listener interpretation
→ reader inference
→ typographic realization
```

> **Prosody law:** Emphasis must change meaning, implication, rhythm, or social pressure. It must never exist merely to make dialogue look expressive.

The same words may carry different propositions depending on stress. Italic emphasis may correct the speaker, polarity, action, actor, transfer, ownership, or object. Typography is selected only after the intended contrast is known.

Available devices include:

- semantic italic stress;
- strategic repetition;
- pause and action beat;
- em-dash interruption;
- restrained ellipsis;
- self-correction;
- restart;
- meaningful fragment;
- echoed word;
- parenthetical afterthought;
- punctuation withdrawal.

> **Parenthetical law:** Parentheses contain secondary pressure inside the primary statement—qualification, reluctant precision, defensive afterthought, or information made smaller. They may not merely explain delivery.

Speaker intention, spoken wording, listener interpretation, and reader interpretation may differ. The engine preserves those differences when they create tension, intimacy, deception, or dramatic irony.

Dialogue rhythm belongs to the character. Emphasis habits, interruption patterns, verbal defenses, pauses, and sentence damage may not be distributed uniformly across the cast.

Under shock, rage, fear, or grief, language may become less elegant. Perfect eloquence requires character justification. Silence may complete the line.

### Dialogue restraint

- no italic inflation;
- no emotional underlining without semantic contrast;
- no transcript literalism;
- no parenthetical clutter;
- no punctuation acting;
- no universal dialogue rhythm;
- no explanation of subtext after it has been performed.

## Narrator Silence Gate

> The narrator withdraws when interpretation would steal ownership of an experience.

The narrator becomes precise when silence would allow a person to disappear.

Silence means reduced interpretation, humor, metaphor, and explanation—not missing sensory truth.

## Metaphor Selection

Metaphors are selected from shared world ecology, character-specific field, situation, target mood, and sensory relevance.

A metaphor should reveal what the observing mind already knows how to notice.

## Mood and Amplitude

Voice remains stable. Tone responds locally. Mood accumulates through path dependence.

The engine performs mood through expectation, interruption, body, material consequence, sentence pressure, information order, and inherited residue. As danger or grief rises, narrator amplitude normally falls.

## Verification

Every rendered scene must pass:

1. canon truth;
2. causal integrity;
3. voice identity;
4. lens transformation;
5. mood-path continuity;
6. humor ethics;
7. dialogue semantic focus;
8. character-specific dialogue rhythm;
9. typographic restraint;
10. subtext preservation;
11. silence discipline;
12. reader trust;
13. selective realization;
14. baseline-adhesion detection.

## Expanded Failure Taxonomy

### Voice
Voice dropout, overamplitude, replacement, lens takeover, wrong-book beauty.

### RCR
Decoration, under-extraction, canon drift, analysis leakage, over-realization, false inference, path flattening, baseline adhesion.

### Dialogue
Italic inflation, emotional underlining, transcript literalism, parenthetical clutter, universal rhythm, subtext explanation, punctuation acting, perfect-emotion eloquence, and emphasis without semantic contrast.

### Ethics
False balance, premature judgment, excessive forgiveness, cruel wit, punching down, consolation theft, beautiful suffering.

### Reader relationship
Reader instruction, question spam, arbitrary withholding, trust debt, smug omniscience.

### Style
Detached narration, literary self-display, relentless cleverness, generic prose, sensory catalogues.
