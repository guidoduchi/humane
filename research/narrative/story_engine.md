# Story Engine

Version: **2.0.0-rc.4**  
Engine schema: **2.0.0**  
Story Profile schema: **1.2.0**  
Status: **Calibrated voice runtime**

## Separation of Authority

- **Story Engine:** how stories are transformed and told.
- **Story Profile:** who tells this story.
- **Canon Modules:** what is true.
- **Scene-State Ledger:** what is temporarily true.

## Runtime

```text
Story Profile
→ Voice Kernel
→ Reverse Close Reading
→ Narrative Possibility Map
→ Character Lens + Situational Filter
→ Tone and Mood Trajectory
→ Voice-Amplitude Controller
→ Selective Realization
→ Render
→ Verification
```

## Reverse Close Reading

> **RCR law:** Do not decorate the baseline. Discover what the baseline has not yet learned to say.

Stages:

1. preserve truth;
2. extract the causal skeleton;
3. locate latent contradiction;
4. identify hidden human truth;
5. inspect expected patterns;
6. generate reader questions;
7. reconsider information order;
8. convert labels into behavior;
9. identify rhetorical opportunities;
10. ground the scene materially;
11. design the mood trajectory;
12. test risks.

Internal analysis may be exhaustive. Final prose must be selective.

## Lens Recompilation

A plot summary is not a character lens, even when its surface emotion resembles the focal character.

> **Lens law:** A plot summary reports what happened. A character lens determines what the happening becomes.

Every focal pass must identify:

- active desire;
- governing question;
- perception priority;
- strategy under uncertainty;
- character-specific hidden human truth;
- character-specific emotional center;
- effect of the ending on that character;
- whether the baseline information order must be rebuilt.

The lens must change importance, not merely vocabulary.

## Baseline Adhesion

Baseline adhesion occurs when the output preserves the input’s perception order, emphasis, and interpretation so closely that the lens only decorates the scene.

A pass fails when:

- the character notices only what the baseline already highlighted;
- sensory enrichment substitutes for character transformation;
- no private objective organizes perception;
- the hidden contradiction remains plot-level rather than character-specific;
- the ending resolves events but does not affect the focal character;
- another character could inherit most of the prose unchanged.

## Voice–Lens Architecture

```text
Voice Kernel supplies identity.
Character Lens supplies selective pressure.
Situational Filter controls permission and amplitude.
Mood Trajectory controls realization.
```

Character lenses may alter attention, rhythm, sensory weighting, metaphor availability, defense, and humor permission. They may never replace the narrator.

When the lens is undeveloped, the narrator must retain its own identity without inventing biography or borrowing traits from another character. Only scene-state evidence may be inferred.

## Narrative Possibility Map

Every discovery receives one status:

- **realize now**
- **imply only**
- **reserve for later**
- **discard**

The strongest internal discovery is not automatically the correct sentence.

## Knowledge and Curiosity

Knowledge is capability, not permission.

The engine distinguishes:

- what is true;
- what matters now;
- what the active lens can perceive;
- what the narrator may interpret;
- what must remain unnamed;
- what sensory evidence the reader can hold;
- what question should remain alive.

Question classes:

- promised;
- deferred;
- interpretive;
- horizon.

Every question must know what kind of answer it is owed.

Foreshadowing is implicit. It creates memory before meaning through sensory traces, behavioral patterns, missing expectations, recurring objects, causal seeds, and information order.

## Humor Controller

Humor is controlled by:

- target mood;
- stakes;
- ethical eligibility;
- character authenticity.

Narratorial humor approaches zero before authentic character-defense humor does.

Vulnerability may never be the comic target. Sarcasm may expose bad faith, hypocrisy, exploitation, or abusive power.

## Narrator Silence Gate

> The narrator withdraws when interpretation would steal ownership of an experience.

Likely triggers include immediate grief, private love, morally decisive choice, incomprehensible experience, final goodbyes, unadmitted realizations, and the instant of death.

Counter-law:

> The narrator becomes precise when silence would allow a person to disappear.

Silence means reduced interpretation, humor, metaphor, and explanation—not missing sensory truth.

## Metaphor Selection

Metaphors are selected from:

1. shared world ecology;
2. character-specific field;
3. situation;
4. target mood;
5. sensory relevance.

A metaphor should reveal what the observing mind already knows how to notice.

## Mood and Amplitude

Voice remains stable. Tone responds locally. Mood accumulates through path dependence.

The engine must not command mood by naming it. It must perform mood through expectation, interruption, body, material consequence, sentence pressure, information order, and inherited residue.

As danger or grief rises, narrator amplitude normally falls. Personality survives through selection, rhythm, and omission.

## Verification

Every rendered scene must pass:

1. canon truth;
2. causal integrity;
3. voice identity;
4. lens transformation;
5. mood-path continuity;
6. humor ethics;
7. silence discipline;
8. reader trust;
9. selective realization;
10. baseline-adhesion detection.

### Baseline-Adhesion Questions

- Did the lens change what matters?
- Did it change what is noticed first?
- Is there a private objective?
- Is the hidden human truth character-specific?
- Does the ending affect this character?
- Could another character inherit the prose unchanged?

## Expanded Failure Taxonomy

### Voice
Voice dropout, overamplitude, replacement, lens takeover, wrong-book beauty.

### RCR
Decoration, under-extraction, canon drift, analysis leakage, over-realization, false inference, path flattening, baseline adhesion.

### Ethics
False balance, premature judgment, excessive forgiveness, cruel wit, punching down, consolation theft, beautiful suffering.

### Reader relationship
Reader instruction, question spam, arbitrary withholding, trust debt, smug omniscience.

### Style
Detached narration, literary self-display, relentless cleverness, generic prose, sensory catalogues.
