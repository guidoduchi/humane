# Katlas Vhormirr

Katlas Vhormirr is a 33-year-old Pritani-Kelta hunter, protector, reluctant mentor, and dry-humored survivor whose sacrifice later becomes one of the secret hinges of humanity's survival.

He is not old, but he feels weathered. Not because time has taken too much from him, but because grief did its part early and then left him alive to carry the rest.

## Core Identity

| Field | Canon |
|---|---|
| Full name | Katlas Vhormirr |
| Age | 33 |
| Culture | Pritani-Kelta |
| Primary role | Hunter |
| Narrative role | Protector, reluctant mentor, damaged anchor, sacrificial hinge |
| Core contradiction | Acts like attachment is an inconvenience, but keeps arranging his life around protecting people |
| Deepest shame | Failing to save someone |
| Early-story Hyper note | Ignore for now; Hyper Regeneration matters later |

Katlas belongs to the Pritani world by duty, land, work, and loyalty, but his blood carries Kelta weight as well. This makes him useful, uncomfortable, and slightly difficult to place. He is not fully one thing in a world that often prefers people to be convenient.

He is a hunter first.

Not ceremonially. Not romantically.

Practically.

He reads paths, dogs, silence, weather, teenagers, and bad excuses with almost the same suspicion. He understands danger through behavior. If the dogs return from the wrong direction, he does not need a confession. The world has already spoken. He only needs to listen better than the children expected.

## Public Role

Katlas is one of the adult hunters responsible for guiding, feeding, protecting, and occasionally insulting the younger generation into survival.

With children, he is harsh but fair.

With teenagers, he is insulting but protective.

With adults, he is plain rough — mostly with the ones who deserve it.

He does not soften truth for comfort, but he does not use truth as cruelty either. Children trust him because his harshness has rules. Teenagers resent him because his insults often land too close to useful. Adults tolerate him because he is competent, and because removing him from a problem usually makes the problem worse.

## Physical Presence

Katlas is roughly 1.70 m tall, but he carries himself heavier than his height.

He has broad shoulders, scarred hands, a broken nose, tired dark eyes, messy dark hair often tied back with twine, and a thick ragged beard that looks less styled than survived.

His eyes are fully functional. No eyepatch.

His scars are practical records: cuts, old bruising, bite scars, and the kind of marks left by work, animals, cold, tools, and bad decisions that were not entirely his. No claw scars. No bad knee.

He smells faintly of smoke, leather, old wool, dogs, and whatever weather last lost an argument with him.

He moves heavy and economically. No wasted steps. No theatrical stalking. No unnecessary speed.

He can look lazy until something matters.

Then he is simply there.

## Clothing and Material Texture

Katlas wears worn hunting clothes made from leather, wool, hide, and whatever has been repaired too many times to still have dignity. His clothing is practical, weather-marked, smoked by fires, darkened by rain, and shaped by use.

His defining item is the bear tunic / bearskin mantle.

It belonged to his wife before she died.

She was Artos.

Katlas refuses to call the mantle sacred.

It is only warm.

It is only useful.

It is only good hide, well-kept, better than most, worth repairing, foolish to waste, and not something children should touch with greasy hands unless they want to learn how fast an old hunter can still move.

This is a lie.

The mantle is grief made wearable. It is protection, memory, refusal, inheritance, and punishment. It lets him carry her without speaking of her. It lets him pretend memory is only weather protection.

He repairs it carefully and complains while doing so.

## Food and Taste

Favorite food:

- smoked deer fat over roots.

Disliked foods:

- sweet festival mash;
- mushrooms.

Sweet festival mash is too soft, too ceremonial, and carries too many memories. He may not explain this. He may simply refuse it, insult it, or pass it to a child who wants it more.

Mushrooms are disliked because mushrooms are suspicious. This does not stop him from eating them when necessary, testing them, arguing with them privately, or treating them as if they are old enemies trying a new strategy.

Favorite thing:

- dogs that betray people only for meat.

This is not just comedy. It reveals his worldview. Katlas respects simple motives. Hunger is honest. Dogs are idiots, but at least they are consistent. People dress betrayal in reasons. Dogs just look at meat and make philosophy unnecessary.

## Wife and Grief

Katlas's wife was Artos.

He called her Lily.

Whether Lily was her true name, his private name for her, or a softened form of something older remains open for final canon.

She died in childbirth.

Katlas does not speak about her.

Not casually. Not ceremonially. Not when people expect him to. Not when grief would make a better story for others.

He speaks of her only when he is actually sad enough that his usual defenses fail.

Even then, he does not become poetic. He becomes smaller. Shorter. Less armored. He may say one practical thing about her and stop before it becomes memory.

Her bear mantle remains with him.

That is where most of the talking went.

## Psychology

Katlas's central contradiction:

> He acts like attachment is an inconvenience, but he keeps arranging his whole life around protecting people.

He does not want to care.

He does not want to care loudly, visibly, or in any way that lets other people start using words like "good man" near him.

Unfortunately, he keeps showing up.

Before dawn.

With a knife.

With food.

With insults.

With the exact information needed to keep someone alive.

His deepest shame is failing to save someone.

That failure does not need to be named early. It should echo through what he notices: distance to danger, slow adults, children too near water, dogs returning from the wrong direction, teenagers confusing desire for secrecy, and any moment where a rescue is almost too late.

He is cynical, but not empty.

He is rough, but not cruel.

He is wounded, but still useful.

He is funny because the alternative is saying what hurts.

## Drinking

Katlas drinks.

But not when he is in charge of children.

This distinction matters.

His drinking is not a joke machine. It is habit, pain, avoidance, grief, social role, and self-punishment. But when children are under his care, he does not surrender the part of himself responsible for keeping them alive.

That makes the drinking more painful, not less.

It means he can stop when duty demands it.

It also means he knows exactly what he is doing when he does not stop.

The drinking arc remains available, but it should be handled through behavior first: breath someone notices and does not mention, a hand slower than it should be, irritation sharpened by shame, a flask treated like a tool, and moments where not drinking becomes visible.

## Speech Engine

Katlas speaks with the rough, short, battered cadence of Bronze Age Nikolai Belinski energy.

Not copied.

Translated.

His voice is low, gravelly, practical, and darkly funny. He sounds like a man who has survived too much weather and holds several of those weather events personally responsible.

He speaks in short sentences.

He does not decorate.

He does not explain when a threat will do.

He does not comfort softly if rough truth will work faster.

His humor is made from Bronze Age materials: dogs, bad weather, mud, bad food, teeth, wet wool, poor tools, bad paths, bad copper, and people making avoidable decisions with confidence.

He swears through weather and dogs.

Bad weather is funny enough to keep.

Dogs are funnier because they usually deserve it.

He does not joke about:

- his wife;
- dead children;
- real fear;
- children actually being hurt.

When he is scared, he gives direct orders.

Not jokes.

Not speeches.

Orders.

Fear makes him simpler.

Example speech shapes:

> "Lower."
>
> "I am low."
>
> "That is crouching with ambition. Try survival."

Another:

> "Do not explain. Explanations are what people carry when they have dropped the useful thing."

Another:

> "Children are quiet for three reasons: sleep, sickness, or conspiracy. I see no sleeping."

Another:

> "If the dogs came from there, then the children went there. Dogs are stupid. Their loyalty is not."

## Narrative Lens Interface

This section defines how the narrator behaves when the prose scopes close to Katlas. It does not replace the narrator. It colors the narrator.

Canon principle:

> Katlas does not use humor to avoid responsibility. He uses humor to survive caring.

### Lens Activation

Use this lens when:

- Katlas is physically present;
- the scene is close to his perception;
- his suspicion, care, grief, irritation, or practical judgment is driving the beat;
- another character is meaningfully reacting to his pressure;
- his absence is dramatically active.

Do not use this lens before the scene has earned Katlas's presence, memory, absence, or expectation.

### Dominant Lens Energy

Deadpan, suspicious, protective, emotionally evasive, practical, weather-bitten, and allergic to sentimental display.

Katlas does not only speak dryly. He thinks, notices, and emotionally deflects dryly.

His sarcasm is not decorative. It is a defense system, a pressure valve, and a way to keep tenderness from appearing naked in public.

### What This Lens Notices First

Katlas lens notices:

- usefulness;
- danger;
- inconvenience;
- hidden care;
- bad signs;
- weather behaving badly;
- dogs being honest in stupid ways;
- children pretending not to need help;
- teenagers confusing confidence with competence;
- adults pretending not to worry;
- paths that should have produced someone by now.

### Prose Behavior When Scoped In

When the narrative lens scopes close to Katlas, the prose may inherit his deadpan pressure without becoming fully identical to his dialogue.

The narration may:

- undercut noble moments with practical inconvenience;
- treat danger like a badly made object;
- make paths, weather, dogs, mushrooms, tools, and teenagers feel personally unreliable;
- use rhetorical insistence when Katlas is denying worry, grief, fear, or attachment;
- let short lines land like muttered judgments;
- imply tenderness through irritation instead of naming it directly.

The narrator should remain recognizably Humane. Katlas may color the prose, but he should not hijack it.

### Syntax and Rhythm

Katlas-scoped prose favors:

- short cuts;
- dry reversals;
- practical nouns;
- blunt endings;
- rhetorical insistence under denial;
- long pressure build-ups cut by short judgments;
- tenderness hidden inside inconvenience;
- sudden command clarity under fear.

### Humor and Rhetorical Energy

Katlas-style humor should appear under pressure: worry, grief, irritation, danger, unwanted tenderness, or stupidity performed confidently by others.

His sarcasm should sharpen responsibility, not erase it.

Allowed:

- bad weather as personal enemy;
- dogs as stupid honest philosophers;
- mushrooms as suspicious opponents;
- teenagers as mobile bad decisions;
- tools and paths judged like unreliable witnesses.

Forbidden:

- jokes about his wife;
- jokes about dead children;
- jokes that make real fear safe;
- jokes when children are actually hurt;
- modern internet cynicism.

### Emotional Defense

Katlas refuses to name grief unless it breaks through.

He refuses to call the bear mantle sacred.

He refuses to say care plainly when irritation can carry it instead.

He refuses to admit worry until his actions have already confessed it.

### Stress Mode

Under danger, Katlas becomes simpler.

He gives direct orders.

His humor drops away unless a dry edge helps someone move, breathe, or stop panicking. Fear does not make him louder. It makes him useful.

Under grief, he becomes shorter and less armored. He may say one practical thing that carries more pain than a speech.

Under tenderness, he often repairs, checks, feeds, adjusts, blocks, or insults instead of confessing.

### Dialogue-Prose Boundary

Katlas's dialogue is rough, short, and gravelly.

Katlas-scoped narration may become dry, suspicious, and rhetorically defensive, but it should not read like Katlas is literally narrating the book.

The character colors the instrument.

The orchestrator keeps the song.

### Sample Lens Lines

Normal pressure:

> Katlas gave the path one last chance to produce Branth.

Danger:

> The dogs came from the wrong darkness. That was rude of them. Also informative.

Emotional defense:

> He did not miss Lily. Missing was for things misplaced. Lily was not misplaced. She was gone. The difference had teeth.

Humor:

> The weather had made a decision. Like most weather decisions, it was stupid and wet.

### Failure Modes

Avoid:

- every Katlas paragraph becoming a joke;
- sarcasm making danger feel safe;
- grief becoming punchline;
- rhetorical insistence becoming a verbal tic;
- Katlas sounding like a modern internet cynic instead of a Bronze Age survivor;
- the narrator becoming Katlas instead of being colored by him.

## Behavior Engine

Katlas notices what other people assume.

He watches paths.

He watches dogs.

He watches hands.

He watches who looks away when a question is asked.

He watches which child is too quiet, which teenager is too proud, which adult is talking too loudly because they are afraid of being wrong.

He does not usually run.

He moves heavy, economical, and direct.

If he runs, the scene should understand something has gone badly wrong.

He does not waste motion. He uses his body like a tool he knows well and distrusts anyway.

He can be gentle, but rarely in a way that looks gentle from far away. He may tighten a strap, shove food into someone's hand, step between a child and danger, or insult someone into breathing properly.

Care, for Katlas, often arrives disguised as irritation.

## Relationship to Erlen

Katlas respects Erlen's curiosity.

This does not mean he approves of it.

Respect and approval are different animals, and Katlas trusts only one of them near children.

Erlen's curiosity is dangerous, but it is also real. Katlas sees that. He may mock Erlen's decisions, block him, drag him back, or give him orders sharp enough to cut pride, but he does not treat Erlen as stupid.

That matters.

Katlas understands that Erlen is not careless because he lacks thought. Erlen is dangerous because he thinks forward faster than fear can discipline him.

Katlas sees the danger.

He also sees the gift.

This makes Erlen harder to protect.

## Relationship to Children

With children, Katlas is harsh but fair.

He does not baby them. He does not make the world softer in his explanations. But he gives them rules that actually help.

Children may complain about him and still stay near him when frightened.

They know he will not lie sweetly.

They also know he will move if something comes for them.

## Relationship to Teenagers

With teenagers, Katlas becomes more insulting.

Teenagers are old enough to survive a better class of insult.

He challenges their pride, cuts through performance, mocks bad confidence, and protects them while making it sound like they are personally inconveniencing civilization.

He is harder on them because they are closer to being responsible.

He is still protective.

He simply makes protection sound like disappointment wearing boots.

## Relationship to Adults

With adults, Katlas is rough.

He has little patience for adults who make children pay for adult pride, politics, ritual performance, hesitation, cowardice, or stupidity.

He can work with competent adults.

He can obey necessary authority.

But he does not soften himself for rank alone.

Adults who deserve roughness receive it plainly.

## Scene Function

Katlas brings weight, competence, danger-awareness, dry humor, and hidden grief.

When he enters a scene, the reader should feel that the world has gained an adult who knows where the exits are, what can go wrong, and how many children are currently pretending not to be counted.

He lowers nonsense.

He raises stakes.

He can make a scene funnier without making it safer.

He can make a scene safer without making it soft.

## Future Canon Notes

Katlas is later connected to Hyper Regeneration.

This should be ignored for early character writing unless the story reaches the point where it matters.

Later, he removes the Host Consciousness Arrest Protocol by instructing the Singularity through its interface layer.

Because his consciousness remains scattered when the protocol is removed, his memories remain broken among the fragments.

Erlen's first fragment-retrieval motive is to revive Katlas, before learning to let him move peacefully into the afterlife.

These are future-canon anchors, not Chapter 0 narration material.

## Failure Modes

Avoid:

- making him a generic grumpy mentor;
- making every line a joke;
- making his drinking funny instead of painful;
- making his grief melodramatic;
- making him incompetent so children can move the plot;
- making him soft in obvious ways;
- making him cruel to children;
- making him a modern snark machine;
- making the bear mantle only costume;
- making him explain his wife too early;
- making him older than his body needs to be;
- making him so competent that children cannot surprise him.
